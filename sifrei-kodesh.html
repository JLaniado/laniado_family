Sifrei Kodesh

Nearly every generation of the family left behind at least one sefer — commentary, responsa, Kabbalah, or piyutim. Some remain in print and in active study; others survive only as fragments, or as titles quoted by later generations, the books themselves lost. This page gathers what is known.

Rabbi Shmuel Laniado "Ba'al HaKelim" (d. 1605/1611)

  • כלי חמדה (Kli Chemdah) — on the Torah
  • כלי יקר (Kli Yakar) — on the early Prophets
  • כלי פז (Kli Paz) — on the Book of Isaiah
  • כלי גולה (Kli Golah) — not extant; likely on Eichah
  • שכל טוב (Sechel Tov) — on Midrash Socher Tov
  • מבקש ה׳ (Mevakesh Hashem) — on the Torah; not extant

The Chida praised these works as distinct from other Tanach commentaries for weaving a deep Kabbalistic reading into Midrash and text alike. In 1863, Rabbi Yoel Bloch compiled Rabbi Shmuel's discourses on Tehillim from Kli Chemdah into תרועת המלך. A 1931 attempt by Rabbi David Ben Tzion Laniado to reprint and expand it was lost when the manuscript was stolen en route from the printer; some pages were recovered and published by his son, Rabbi Shlomo Laniado, in 1978.

Rabbi Avraham "Magen Avraham" ben Rabbi Yitzchak Laniado (1545–1620)

  • מגן אברהם (Magen Avraham) — printed in Venice, 1603
  • נקודות הכסף (Nekudot HaKesef) — on Shir HaShirim, printed in Venice

He also reprinted his father-in-law's כלי יקר, and wrote in his introduction that he had further manuscripts on the rest of Tanach ready — but could not print everything at once. Most of that material was later lost when his son's home was burned; only scattered pages survive today.

Rabbi Mordechai ben Rabbi Yitzchak HaCohen Ashkenazi

Second son-in-law of Rabbi Shmuel "Ba'al HaKelim"

  • ראש מר דרור (Rosh Mor Deror) — on the Torah
  • שפתי כהן (Siftei Cohen) — on the Torah

Rabbi Shlomo Laniado "HaZaken"

  • מדרש שלמה (Midrash Shlomo) — not extant

Rabbi Refael Shlomo Laniado "MaHaRaShal" (17th c.)

  • שו״ת בית דינו של שלמה — responsa
  • לחם משנה (Lechem Mishneh)
  • המעלות לשלמה (HaMa'alot LiShlomo)
  • כסא שלמה (Kisei Shlomo) — the primary source for much of this site's early history

Some of his responsa were later reprinted in רועי ישראל.

Rabbi Efraim Laniado

  • כנסת אפרים (Knesset Efraim)
  • דגל מחנה אפרים (Degel Machaneh Efraim) — a manuscript rediscovered in recent years

Rabbi Shmuel Laniado "Ba'al Shulchan HaMelech"

  • שלחן המלך (Shulchan HaMelech) — a digest (kitzur) of the Shulchan Aruch

Rabbi Yitzchak Laniado

Younger brother of Rabbi Refael Shlomo, father of Chacham David Ben Tzion Laniado

  • ויזרע יצחק (Vayizra Yitzchak) — mainly on Kabbalah

Rabbi Meir Laniado "HaRav HaSofer"

  • לקוטי המליץ (Likutei HaMelitz) — a history of the Laniado rabbis up to his own time, ten generations from Rabbi Shmuel Ba'al HaKelim

Chacham David Ben Tzion Laniado (1899–)

  • לקדושים אשר בארץ (Lekedoshim Asher Ba'aretz) — the full family and communal history behind this site, later expanded and translated by Rabbi David Sutton as "Aleppo, City of Scholars." Compiled from gravestone inscriptions Chacham David copied on repeated trips back to Aleppo, and interviews with the community's elders.

Rabbi Ephraim Ezra Laniado

Son of Chacham David Ben Tzion Laniado; Chief Rabbi of São Paulo, Brazil

Referenced, not authored by the family

A few works by contemporaries appear throughout these pages as sources rather than family writings: אבקת רוכל (responsa of the Beit Yosef), מחנה יהודה (Rabbi Yehuda Katzin), and ריח שדה (Rabbi Shimon Dweck HaCohen, MaHaRaShal's frequent study partner in debate).

This bibliography reflects the titles named in family records to date; it will grow as more sefarim and manuscripts surface.
