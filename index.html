Guarding a Legacy

שושלת משפחת לניאדו

For over four centuries, the Laniado (לניאדו) family produced Chief Rabbis, Dayanim (rabbinic judges), and Torah scholars — first in Aleppo, Syria, and later across Israel, Egypt, Europe, and the Americas. This site gathers that history: the people, the books they wrote, the stories told about them, and the family that carries their name today.

From the Iberian expulsion of 1492 to five continents today.

Where the Story Begins

The Laniado family traces its roots to the Iberian Peninsula, before the Spanish Inquisition and the expulsion of 1492. The name לניאדו (Laniado) has two competing explanations: some say it comes from a word for "one who deals in wool or hair," while others trace it to linaje — Castilian for "lineage" or "tree."

The first documented member of the family, Rabbi Shmuel Laniado HaRishon (ר׳ שמואל לניאדו הראשון, "the First," d. 1533), left Spain around the time of the expulsion, traveled through Edirne (Andrinople) in what is today Turkey, and eventually settled in Aleppo, Syria — known in Hebrew as ארם צובא (Aram Tzova). From that point on, Aleppo became the family's home for over three hundred years, though individual members also lived and taught Torah in Egypt, Iraq, the Land of Israel, Turkey, Greece, the Netherlands, and Italy.

Over the following generations, the Laniado family produced more than 44 documented Chief Rabbis, rabbis, and Dayanim — an unbroken thread of Torah scholarship that historians and family alike have worked to preserve.
