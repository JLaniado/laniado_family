Laniado Hospital
Netanya, Israel · Founded 1975

Not every Laniado story is about Aleppo, rabbinic succession, or the 16th century. One of the most visible living uses of the family name today belongs to a hospital in Netanya, Israel — and it reaches the family through a different route entirely: Swiss banking, not the rabbinate.

A Rebbe's Response to Loss

Laniado Hospital was founded by the Klausenberger Rebbe, Rabbi Yekutiel Yehuda Halberstam. After losing his wife and eleven children in the Holocaust, he devoted the rest of his life to rebuilding — first a religious community in Israel, and then a hospital that would operate according to halacha: kosher food as standard, no unnecessary desecration of Shabbat, and care extended to patients of every background.

The Laniado Family's Role

Alphonse and Yaakov Avraham Laniado were wealthy Swiss businessmen who managed a family bank in Switzerland. In 1975, they gave the Klausenberger Rebbe the hospital's first major donation — $300,000 (equivalent to roughly $1.7 million in 2024 terms). They later left a majority of their estate to the hospital in their wills, cementing their name to the institution that carries it today.

A hospital built from one family's unimaginable loss, sustained in part by another family's generosity — both carrying forward, in their own way, a history of care for the Jewish community.
