The Diaspora

For most of its documented history, the Laniado family's story is the story of one city: Aleppo. That changed in a single generation. Chacham David Ben Tzion Laniado (1899–) — who left Aleppo for the Land of Israel at 18, survived the 1929 Hebron riots, and spent decades documenting the family's history — had fourteen children. They, and their own children after them, carried the name to Israel, Brazil, Italy, the United States, and beyond. This page follows that scattering.

For his own story and sources, see his profile within the Family Tree. What follows is where his children settled.

A Family Still in Touch With Its Root

What stands out across these branches is not distance but continuity: sons who became rabbis, Torah teachers, and communal leaders on three continents; daughters who married into other scholarly families; descendants who still return, in memory or in person, to the world their grandfather Chacham David spent his life recording. The family that once served nearly unbroken as Chief Rabbis of one city now has roots in Jerusalem, Tel Aviv, Ashdod, São Paulo, Deal, and Brooklyn — five branches of the same tree.

This page currently covers Chacham David's generation and its immediate settling points. A fuller picture — grandchildren, great-grandchildren, and other 20th-century branches of the family beyond his direct line — will be added as more records come in. See Sources & Contribute to help fill it in.
