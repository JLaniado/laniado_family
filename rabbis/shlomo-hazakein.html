ר׳ שלמה לניאדו ״הזקן״
Rabbi Shlomo Laniado, "HaZaken" (the Elder)
Mid–late 16th century · Generation 5 · Chief Rabbi of Aleppo

Rabbi Shlomo was the eldest of six sons of Rabbi Avraham "HaMusmach," and took over as Chief Rabbi of Aleppo after his father. He is called ״הזקן״ ("the Elder") to distinguish him from a grandson and great-great-grandson who shared his name.

Rabbi Yehuda Katzin, in his work מחנה יהודה, wrote of him:

"Rabbi Shlomo HaZaken was at a level where the Divine Presence could have come to him directly, but the generation was not ready for it... In my time, before anyone would say any Torah insight, they would first go to Rabbi Shlomo HaZaken."

Katzin also records a responsum in which Rabbi Moshe ben Chaviv cited a ruling of Rabbi Shlomo HaZaken alongside other great sages of the era, noting that everyone discussing it spoke with great respect and were reluctant to disagree with him. Much of Rabbi Shlomo's own Torah writing, unfortunately, was not preserved by a generation that — as Katzin laments — did not sufficiently value the Torah being produced at the time.

He is credited with a work called מדרש שלמה (Midrash Shlomo), which is not extant today.

The meeting with Shabbtai Tzvi

When Shabbtai Tzvi passed through Syria, he met with Rabbi Shlomo, who — like many great rabbis of that era — initially believed he might be the long-awaited Messiah. For reasons the sources do not explain, Rabbi Shlomo withdrew his support before the wider Jewish world recognized the movement as false, and urged others to stop following Shabbtai Tzvi early — sparing the Aleppo community some of the disillusionment that followed elsewhere.

His brothers

Rabbi Shlomo had five younger brothers. His brother Rabbi Levi Laniado became rabbi in Alexandria, Egypt; his son, Rabbi Shmuel ben Rabbi Levi ("Rav HaMuvhak"), succeeded him there and was praised by Rabbi Shlomo's own son as fluent in the entire Talmud — Yerushalmi, Bavli, and beyond. Another brother, Rabbi Yitzchak Laniado, oversaw a new printing of כלי פז. Little is known of the remaining three sons.
