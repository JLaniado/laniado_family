ר׳ שמואל לניאדו הראשון
Rabbi Shmuel Laniado HaRishon ("the First")
Died 1533 · Generation 1 · The family's founder in Aleppo

Rabbi Shmuel Laniado HaRishon is the earliest documented member of the family. He left Spain around the time of the 1492 expulsion and the Inquisition that followed, traveling first to Edirne (called Andrinopol Togarmah in the sources), in what is today Turkey, before eventually settling in Aleppo — ארם צובא (Aram Tzova) — in Syria.

Little is recorded of his life. His grandson, Rabbi Refael Shlomo Laniado, wrote in his own work כסא שלמה (Kisei Shlomo, "Throne of Shlomo"):

"The root of our family is from those who fled from the Inquisition — we do not know at which point during the Inquisition, or from where exactly. But the Ra'am brings a halachic question and says, 'that the sage, our teacher Rabbi Shmuel Laniado, is no longer here [in Turkey]' — so we see he was a great rabbi."

An unpublished manuscript by Rabbi Aharon Geresh, a contemporary, similarly records: "I heard from the mouth of the elder sage, Laniado, of blessed memory" — though it is unclear whether this refers to Rabbi Shmuel HaRishon himself, his grandson Rabbi Shmuel "Ba'al HaKelim," or his son Rabbi Avraham.

What is known is that Rabbi Shmuel HaRishon had at least two sons: Rabbi Avraham Laniado "HaDarshan" and Rabbi Moshe, who together signed a letter expressing their attachment to the Land of Israel — a document that would echo through the family for generations.

On the sources: Much of what we know about the earliest generations comes from later family members writing about their ancestors — particularly Rabbi Refael Shlomo Laniado's כסא שלמה. Dates before the 17th century should be treated as approximate.
