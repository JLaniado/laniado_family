The Chief Rabbis of Aleppo

In the mid-16th century, the community of Aleppo wrote to Rabbi Yosef Karo — author of the שולחן ערוך (Shulchan Aruch) and known as the Beit Yosef — asking him to become their Chief Rabbi. Too occupied to go himself, he sent instead a man he called "ish asher kamoni" — איש אשר כמוני, "a man who is like me": Rabbi Shmuel Laniado. Although the native families of Aleppo had a tradition of appointing their Chief Rabbi only from בית דוד (the House of David), the Beit Yosef's recommendation was enough — Rabbi Shmuel was accepted, and for the next six generations, his descendants held the position almost without interruption.

Below is that chain, from the family's arrival in Aleppo through the last Laniado to hold the title of Chief Rabbi there. Click through in order, or jump to any generation.

After Rabbi Efraim, the family continued producing Dayanim and Torah scholars in Aleppo and, later, in the Land of Israel — including Chacham David Ben Tzion Laniado, the family's historian. See the Family Tree for that fuller picture.

Sources include כסא שלמה (Kisei Shlomo), מחנה יהודה (Machaneh Yehuda), the introductions to the family's printed sefarim, and testimony recorded in לקדושים אשר בארץ (Aleppo, City of Scholars).
