ר׳ אברהם לניאדו ״הרב הדרשן״
Rabbi Avraham Laniado, "HaDarshan" (the Preacher)
Born 1586 · Generation 2 · Son of the founder

Rabbi Avraham was the son of Rabbi Shmuel Laniado HaRishon. He is mentioned several times in the writings of his own son, Rabbi Shmuel "Ba'al HaKelim," who quotes him directly — ״ואומר בדרושי אבא מרי ז״ל״ ("and I say, in the discourses of my father, my teacher, of blessed memory..."). It appears he authored a sefer of his own — the later work כלי חמדה (Kli Chemdah) cites his father's homilies — but that book has not survived to today.

In 1537 he wrote a letter expressing his love for the Land of Israel and his wish to move there, signed together with his brother Rabbi Moshe. He did, in fact, live in Tzfat (Safed) for a period of time: his son, the "Ba'al HaKelim," would later quote teachings he heard directly — through his father — from the great sages who were in Tzfat at that time, among them Maran Beit Yosef, the Ari HaKadosh, the RaMaK, and RaChaVo.

Rabbi Avraham had two sons: Rabbi Shmuel "Ba'al HaKelim", who would go on to become Chief Rabbi of Aleppo, and Rabbi Yitzchak, about whom less is known — though Rabbi Tzadka Chutzin describes him as ״החכם השלם והכולל כמהר״ר יצחק לניאדו נר״ו״ ("the complete and comprehensive sage, our honored teacher Rabbi Yitzchak Laniado"), a mark of high regard.
