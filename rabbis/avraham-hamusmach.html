רב אברהם לניאדו ״הרב המוסמך״
Rabbi Avraham Laniado, "HaMusmach" (the Ordained)
Died 1646 · Generation 4 · Chief Rabbi of Aleppo, ~40 years

Rabbi Avraham was the son of Rabbi Shmuel "Ba'al HaKelim." He earned the title ״הרב המוסמך״ — "the Ordained Rabbi" — because he received semicha (rabbinic ordination) directly from the Beit Yosef and his own students, connecting him personally to the great circle of Tzfat scholarship his father had studied under. He succeeded his father as Chief Rabbi of Aleppo and, like him, held the position for roughly 40 years.

His son, Rabbi Shlomo, wrote of him in the introduction to a new printing of כלי פז:

"Beside being a massive Torah scholar, he was wealthy and respected in the community, and spent his life spreading Torah to everyone."

Beyond this, little else is recorded of Rabbi Avraham's life — a reminder that even a Chief Rabbi who held the position for four decades can leave a relatively thin paper trail, especially compared to descendants whose own students and children wrote about them at length.

A different member of the same generation — Rabbi Avraham "Magen Avraham" ben Rabbi Yitzchak Laniado (1545–1620), who married his own niece, a daughter of Rabbi Shmuel "Ba'al HaKelim" — is a distinct figure, not to be confused with Rabbi Avraham "HaMusmach." He printed his own sefer, מגן אברהם, in Venice in 1603, and reprinted several of his father-in-law's works. His story is told in full on the Family Tree page.
