ר׳ אפרים לניאדו
Rabbi Efraim Laniado
Generation 8 · The last Chief Rabbi of the Laniado line, 18 years

Rabbi Efraim was the son of Rabbi Refael Shlomo, and took over communal leadership when his father grew ill — seven years before his father's passing. Because he served as a Dayan even at a young age, he was known as ״הרב הדיין״ ("the Rabbi-Judge"). He was also a mekubal and a payetan, and was instrumental in printing and compiling many sefarim of his generation.

His own works include ספר כנסת אפרים, and a more recently rediscovered manuscript, ספר דגל מחנה אפרים.

When Rabbi Efraim first began serving as Dayan, some in the community protested that he was not yet as worthy as his father and should not be followed. His father, Rabbi Shlomo, wrote in his defense that Rabbi Efraim was fluent in the entire Torah and fully fit to be Chief Rabbi — and that whatever he ruled should be followed as practical halacha. Rabbi Moshe Galanti likewise affirmed that he was "more than fit, and very worthy" to succeed his father.

Rabbi Efraim served as Chief Rabbi for 18 years and left children behind him — but his tenure marks the end of an unbroken line: he was the last Chief Rabbi of Aleppo from the Laniado family. The position would pass outside the family after him.

The family's Torah scholarship did not end here. Descendants continued to serve as Dayanim and sages in Aleppo for generations afterward, and — through a different branch of the family — the line eventually reaches Chacham David Ben Tzion Laniado, who documented this entire history and whose own children carried the Laniado name to Israel, Brazil, Italy, and the United States.
