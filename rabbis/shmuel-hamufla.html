Rabbi Shmuel Laniado, "HaMufla" (the Extraordinary)
Generation 6 · Chief Rabbi of Aleppo, ~40 years

Sources differ on whether Rabbi Shmuel "HaMufla" was the son of Rabbi Avraham "HaMusmach" or of Rabbi Shlomo "HaZaken" — either way, he studied under Rabbi Shlomo HaZaken and went on to serve as Chief Rabbi of Aleppo for roughly 40 years. His son wrote of him that "he was a great anav (humble man) and did great kindness to all."

Rabbi Yehuda Tzadka wrote of him:

"He taught halacha to the world, and the world begged to learn from him. Fluent in the entire Talmud, there is only one like him in the generation. He is our father, our king, and our rabbi."

When the yeshiva in Babylon (Iraq) fell on difficult times and needed support, Rabbi Shmuel would send his own students there — and, in turn, students from Babylon would come to study in Aleppo.

Visited by the Shelah HaKadosh

The Shelah HaKadosh, traveling from Poland toward the Land of Israel, stopped in Syria and stayed roughly three weeks — including through the Yamim Nora'im (High Holy Days). He later wrote:

"When I arrived in Syria and looked around at all the Torah, I said, blessed is God — the people here are learning all day and sleep very little at night, fulfilling mitzvot all day. And the Chief Rabbi, Rabbi Shmuel Laniado, is a Torah scholar in the entire revealed and hidden Torah — there wasn't a single teaching or allusion he didn't know."

Five sons

When Rabbi Shmuel "HaMufla" passed away, he left five sons:

  • Rabbi Shlomo, the eldest, "Ba'al Beit Dino Shel Shlomo," who succeeded him as leader of Aleppo.
  • Rabbi Avraham, father-in-law of Rabbi Michael Harari, whose two responsa appear in בית דינו של שלמה.
  • Rabbi Yitzchak, one of whose responsa is cited by Rabbi Tzadka Chutzin.
  • Rabbi Eliyahu, who served on the Beit Din of Aleppo and is remembered for his piety and Torah.
  • Rabbi David, the youngest, also a scholar in revealed and hidden Torah, and a member of Aleppo's Beit Din.
